# AI and Data Usage Policy

- **Status:** Proposed baseline for organizational approval
- **Applies to:** Employees and contractors using AI tools for work
- **Owner:** Organization-designated security or IT owner
- **Review:** At least annually and when approved AI tools or data rules change

## Purpose

Use AI tools productively without disclosing information the organization, its customers, or its partners expect to remain private. This policy applies to prompts, uploads, connected repositories, plugins, and tool integrations.

## Never enter into an AI tool

Unless the organization has explicitly approved a specific tool and workflow for that data, never paste, upload, or connect:

- Passwords, API keys, access tokens, private keys, recovery codes, session cookies, or other credentials.
- Confidential or restricted source code, configuration, database exports, logs, or architecture details.
- Personal, employee, applicant, customer, or vendor information that identifies a person or account.
- Customer/vendor contracts, pricing, transactions, payment details, support cases, or other non-public records.
- Unreleased financial, legal, strategic, security, incident, or business information.
- Data labeled confidential/restricted or information you are not authorized to disclose.

Removing names is not enough if remaining details could identify a person, organization, account, or incident.

## Allowed use

- Use only AI tools and accounts approved by the organization for work.
- Public information and synthetic examples may be used when the tool is approved and the content is appropriate.
- Share the smallest amount of information needed. Prefer fictional data, redacted examples, and high-level descriptions.
- Do not connect an AI tool to a repository, cloud drive, email, or ticket system without explicit organizational approval.
- Review AI-generated code and content for correctness, security, licensing, and confidential information before use or publication. A human remains responsible for the result.
- Follow stricter contractual, legal, customer, and organizational rules when they apply.

## If information may have been exposed

Stop sharing the material, report it promptly to the organization's security or IT contact, and follow their incident instructions. If a credential was exposed, have its owner revoke or rotate it immediately. Do not rely on deleting a prompt or repository commit as proof that the information is gone.

## Approval and exceptions

This document is a baseline proposal, not evidence of formal organizational approval. The designated owner must approve the policy, identify approved AI tools, and define any permitted confidential-data workflows before staff rely on exceptions. Ask the owner before sharing information when its classification or the tool's approval is unclear.
